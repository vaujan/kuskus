A few practical details

Before you make it yours.

Can I use my existing Obsidian vault?

Yes. Kusu can index an existing Markdown folder or Obsidian vault without converting its notes. Core wikilinks, aliases, heading links, and note and image embeds are supported. Obsidian plugins and every plugin-specific format are not part of that compatibility.

Does Kusu change my Markdown files?

Opening a note or switching editing modes does not rewrite its Markdown. Edits you make in Kusu save to the actual file. Existing YAML properties remain user-authored content; Kusu keeps its own metadata in a hidden .kuskus folder and leaves .obsidian alone.

What works offline?

Your local notes, images, and files remain available offline. Opening a website, fetching a link preview, or displaying a remote image needs a connection and contacts the relevant host.

How do I back up my library?

Close the app, then copy the entire vault folder, including .kuskus. Keeping the hidden folder preserves Kusu’s metadata alongside your notes and attachments.

Which platforms are ready?

Windows is the first public installer target. macOS and Linux builds are experimental. Check release notes and available assets for the status of each build before installing.

Is Kusu free and open source?

Yes. Kusu is licensed under GPL-3.0-only. You can use, inspect, modify, and redistribute it under that license. No account is required.
